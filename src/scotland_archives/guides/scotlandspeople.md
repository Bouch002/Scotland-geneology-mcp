# ScotlandsPeople — Research Guide

**URL:** https://www.scotlandspeople.gov.uk
**Operated by:** National Records of Scotland (NRS)

---

## What ScotlandsPeople Is

ScotlandsPeople is the official Scottish government genealogy website, jointly
managed by NRS, the Court of the Lord Lyon, and the Registrar General for Scotland.
It is the primary access point for digitised Scottish genealogy records held by NRS.

It is not a free service — you pay per page view (credits) or buy an annual subscription.
Index searches (seeing that a record exists) are free; downloading images costs credits.

---

## Record Categories

### People Records
All records linked to named individuals:
- OPR Births & Baptisms (c.1553–1854)
- OPR Marriages & Banns (c.1553–1854)
- OPR Deaths & Burials (c.1553–1854)
- Statutory Births (1855–present, recent restricted)
- Statutory Marriages (1855–present)
- Statutory Deaths (1855–present, recent restricted)
- Census (1841, 1851, 1861, 1871, 1881, 1891, 1901, 1911, 1921)
- Wills & Testaments (1513–1925)
- Valuation Rolls (1855–1989)
- Catholic Parish Registers (c.1703–1993)
- Church Records — non-OPR denominations
- Prison Registers (1657–1939)
- Soldiers' & Airmen's Wills
- Poor Relief & Migration Records
- Coats of Arms (1672–present)
- Divorce Records (1984–present)

### Places Records
Ordnance Survey Name Books — the field notes made during the original OS surveys.
Useful for locating named farms and settlements that appear on 19th-century maps.

### Virtual Volumes
Digital reproductions of key reference works:
- Old Statistical Account of Scotland (1791–1799)
- New Statistical Account of Scotland (1834–1845)
- Third Statistical Account of Scotland (1951–1992)

These parish-by-parish surveys are invaluable for understanding the economy,
society, and geography of a specific parish at a given period.

## Searching Effectively

### Surname Variants
Scottish surnames — particularly Gaelic-origin names — had no standardised spelling
until the late 19th century. Key patterns:

- Mac / Mc / M' prefixes are interchangeable (MacDonald = McDonald = M'Donald)
- Final consonants were often softened or dropped in Anglicised forms
- Norse-origin Shetland and Orkney names often have unique local variants
- Use the wildcard search (`%`) for uncertain middle letters

### OPR Coverage Gaps
Not all parishes recorded births, marriages, and deaths equally. Before concluding
a record is missing:
1. Check the ScotlandsPeople parish catalogue (free) for the exact date coverage
2. Check the parent parish — some parishes were sub-divided after the OPR period started
3. Consider whether the family may have been Dissenting (no OPR record)

### Census Year Strategy
Always search all available census years, not just one:
- A person aged 30 in 1851 should appear in 1841 (aged ~20), 1861 (aged ~40), etc.
- Children leave the parental home — find them in later censuses with their own families
- Widows often moved in with children — the 1901 or 1911 census may show an elderly
  parent living with adult children

### Statutory Register Tips
The statutory registers (post-1855) are the most information-rich records. A death
certificate names the deceased's parents — even if the deceased was elderly — giving
you a generation of detail without ever searching OPR.

## Complementary Sources

ScotlandsPeople does not hold everything. For records not yet digitised:
- **NRS search rooms (Edinburgh):** Original registers, court records, maps
- **Local archives:** Burgh records, sheriff court processes, estate papers
- **Archives Hub:** Finding aids for institutional collections across Scotland
- **NLS:** Books, newspapers, maps, manuscripts
