# Scottish Genealogy Record Types — Reference Guide

---

## Church of Scotland — Old Parish Registers (OPR)

**Coverage:** c.1553–1854 | **ScotlandsPeople record type:** opr_births, opr_marriages, opr_deaths

The OPR are the primary pre-civil registration source for Scotland. They were kept
by individual Church of Scotland parishes and vary enormously in quality, completeness,
and start date.

**Births & Baptisms**
- Record the child's name, father's name, mother's name (maiden name often given), date
- Many early records give only the father's name
- Illegitimate births sometimes recorded, often with a note on the father's identity
- Gaps are common — if a baptism is missing, the family may have been Dissenting

**Marriages & Banns**
- Banns (proclamations of intent) were posted over three consecutive Sundays
- Both sets of parents sometimes named; witnesses often relatives
- Irregular marriages (not in church) are rarely in the OPR

**Deaths & Burials**
- The least complete category — many parishes barely recorded burials
- Where recorded: name, sometimes age or spouse's name
- Absence of a death record does not mean the person didn't die in that parish

**Searching tips:**
- Surname spelling was not standardised — try Mac/Mc/M', and common phonetic variants
- Check the ScotlandsPeople parish catalogue (free) for coverage dates before concluding records are missing
- Some parishes were carved out of older ones — check parent parishes for earlier records

## Civil / Statutory Registers

**Coverage:** 1855–present | **ScotlandsPeople record types:** stat_births, stat_marriages, stat_deaths

From 1 January 1855, all births, marriages, and deaths in Scotland were required to
be registered with the local Registrar.

**Births (from 1855)**
- Child's name, date and place of birth
- Father's name, occupation, and birthplace
- Mother's name (maiden surname), birthplace
- Date and place of parents' marriage
- Informant's name and relationship

**Marriages (from 1855)**
- Full names, ages, and occupations of both parties
- Both sets of parents named (father's occupation given)
- Witnesses named

**Deaths (from 1855)**
- Name, age, occupation, cause of death
- Usual residence
- Parents' names (even for elderly decedents — often the only record of pre-1855 parents)
- Name of informant

**Access restrictions:** Records within approximately the last 100 years have restricted
access. Check the ScotlandsPeople website for current cutoff dates.

## Census Records

**Coverage:** 1841, 1851, 1861, 1871, 1881, 1891, 1901, 1911, 1921

Scottish census records from 1841 onwards survive almost entirely (unlike England,
where bombing destroyed some London records). Taken every 10 years on a specific night.

**1841:** Names, approximate ages (adults rounded down to nearest 5), occupation,
birthplace (county or "Y" for Scotland)

**1851 onwards:** Name, relationship to head of household, marital status, age,
occupation, parish and county of birth

**Research tips:**
- Families often had lodgers or boarders who may be relatives
- Address in census ties to valuation rolls for year-by-year tracking
- Use multiple census years — family composition changes reveal births, deaths, marriages

## Wills & Testaments

**Coverage:** 1513–1925

Scottish law of succession differs from English law. The pre-1823 system used
Commissary Courts; after 1823, Sheriff Courts.

**Testament Dative:** Appointed an executor when no will was left (intestacy)
**Testament Testamentar:** Recorded the terms of an actual will

Contents typically include: name of deceased, executor names, inventory of moveable
property (not land), debts owed and owing.

**Research value:** Names executors and beneficiaries — often relatives — mapping
the wider family network. Occupational detail can help distinguish individuals with
common names.

## Valuation Rolls

**Coverage:** 1855–1989 (annual)

Annual assessment of every property in Scotland, listing the occupier, proprietor,
and annual value. Published annually between census years — effectively a near-annual
directory of where everyone lived.

**Research value:**
- Track a family's address year by year between censuses
- Identify when a family arrived in or left a parish
- Distinguish between property owners (proprietors) and renters (occupiers)
- Useful for urban areas where directory listings are incomplete

---

## Catholic Parish Registers

**Coverage:** c.1703–1993

Registers kept by Roman Catholic parishes. Coverage is uneven:
- Lowland industrial towns (Glasgow, Dundee, Coatbridge) — reasonable coverage from
  the 1840s, reflecting Irish immigration
- Highland and Hebridean parishes — often significant gaps, some registers lost
- Available on ScotlandsPeople; some also via local diocesan archives

## Non-Established Church Records

**Coverage:** Varies, c.1700s–1900s

Following the 1843 Disruption, nearly a third of the Church of Scotland ministry
left to form the Free Church. Other Dissenting denominations also kept records.

**Denominations covered on ScotlandsPeople:**
- Free Church of Scotland (post-1843)
- United Presbyterian Church
- Reformed Presbyterian (Covenanting) Church
- Scottish Episcopal Church

**Research tip:** If an ancestor cannot be found in OPR, consider whether they were
Dissenting. Areas with strong Covenanting traditions (south-west Scotland) and
areas with significant Episcopal presence (north-east Scotland) are particularly
likely to have non-OPR ancestors.

## Other Record Types

| Type | Coverage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Prison Registers | 1657–1939 | Birthplace, occupation, physical description |
| Poor Relief | c.1700s–1900s | Kirk Session records; removal orders show place of origin |
| Valuation Rolls | 1855–1989 | Annual; tracks address between censuses |
| Coats of Arms | 1672–present | Lord Lyon; legally binding in Scotland |
| Soldiers' Wills | Various | Brief field wills; cross-reference with TNA for full records |
| Divorces | 1984–present | Sheriff Court; pre-1984 at Court of Session |
